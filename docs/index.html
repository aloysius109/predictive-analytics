READ THE ESTIMATE IN CONTEXT

What the model can tell you.

Historical patterns provide a starting point. Changes in policy, conflict, decision practice or who applies can change future outcomes.

What do the outcomes mean?

Grant: recognition as a refugee. Other protection: complementary protection. Refusal: a rejected claim. Closure: an otherwise-closed decision without a substantive grant or refusal. All four are included in the main probability distribution.

How is the model trained?

Multinomial logistic regression uses country-of-origin, country-of-asylum and country-pair effects, plus time trends. Outcome counts weight the training data. Cases and persons use separate models, with person-based estimates preferred where there is coverage. Authorities and procedural stages are pooled.

The decision year is when the outcome is recorded, not when an application was submitted. Decisions may include appeals; these records do not represent unique applicants.

How well does it predict?

On held-out 2025 person-based decisions, log loss was 0.9467, compared with 1.0707 for the historical country-pair baseline: approximately 11.6% lower. Lower log loss is better. This is one test year, not a guarantee of future accuracy.

The case-based model performed worse than its baseline and is flagged as experimental. The model does not provide confidence intervals. Small or unseen country pairs have greater uncertainty.

Where do the data come from?

UNHCR’s asylum-decision statistics, using the 2000–2025 snapshot bundled in the project. The four component outcome counts define the denominator. Rows with non-country codes and zero totals are excluded. The data are not refreshed automatically.
